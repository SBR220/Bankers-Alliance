QUESTION: The Unified Payments Interface (UPI), which allows instant 24x7 bank-to-bank money transfer through a single mobile app, was developed by which organisation?
A. Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
B. National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI)
C. Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)
D. National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD)
E. Indian Banks' Association (IBA)
ANSWER: B
SOLUTION: <b>✅ Correct answer: B – National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI)</b>

UPI is a real-time payment system built and operated by <b>NPCI</b>. Users send or receive money instantly, <mark>24x7 – even on bank holidays</mark>, using a <b>Virtual Payment Address (VPA)</b> such as <i>name@bank</i>, without sharing bank account details. Every payment is approved with a secret <b>UPI PIN</b>.

<b>📌 Key pointers</b>
<ul><li><b>NPCI</b> is the umbrella organisation for retail payments in India – set up in <u>2008</u> by the <b>RBI</b> with the support of the <b>Indian Banks' Association (IBA)</b>.</li><li>It is a <b>not-for-profit company</b> (Section 8, Companies Act 2013) with its headquarters in <b>Mumbai</b>.</li><li>UPI was launched in <mark>2016</mark>; the <b>BHIM</b> app (NPCI's own UPI app) followed in December 2016.</li><li><b>UPI 123PAY</b> lets feature-phone users make UPI payments without internet.</li></ul>

<b>🧠 Why not the other options?</b>
<ul><li><b>RBI</b> – the <i>regulator</i> of payment systems (Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007). It promoted NPCI but did not build UPI itself.</li><li><b>SEBI</b> – regulates the securities (share) market, not payments.</li><li><b>NABARD</b> – apex development bank for agriculture and rural development.</li><li><b>IBA</b> – an association of banks; it supported the creation of NPCI but does not run UPI.</li></ul>

<b>📊 NPCI products at a glance</b>
<table><tr><th>Product</th><th>Full form</th><th>What it does</th></tr><tr><td><b>UPI</b></td><td>Unified Payments Interface</td><td>Instant bank-to-bank transfer using a VPA, 24x7</td></tr><tr><td><b>IMPS</b></td><td>Immediate Payment Service</td><td>Instant inter-bank transfer, 24x7</td></tr><tr><td><b>RuPay</b></td><td>Rupee + Payment</td><td>India's own domestic card network (alternative to Visa / Mastercard)</td></tr><tr><td><b>NACH</b></td><td>National Automated Clearing House</td><td>Bulk and recurring payments – salaries, EMIs, subsidies</td></tr><tr><td><b>BBPS</b></td><td>Bharat Bill Payment System</td><td>One platform to pay electricity, water, DTH and other bills</td></tr><tr><td><b>AePS</b></td><td>Aadhaar Enabled Payment System</td><td>Basic banking using Aadhaar + fingerprint</td></tr><tr><td><b>NETC FASTag</b></td><td>National Electronic Toll Collection</td><td>Automatic toll payment on highways</td></tr></table>

<b>⚡ Quick memory trick:</b> <mark>RBI = Regulator, NPCI = Operator.</mark> If a question asks who runs or developed UPI, RuPay, IMPS or BBPS, the answer is <b>NPCI</b>.

TEXT: <b>Consider the following statements about Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) in India:</b>
&#8203;
I. RRBs were established under the Regional Rural Banks Act, 1976.
II. The ownership of an RRB is shared among the Central Government, the State Government and the Sponsor Bank in the ratio 50 : 15 : 35.
III. The setting up of RRBs was recommended by the Narasimham Working Group (1975).
IV. RRBs are regulated and supervised by SEBI.
QUESTION: Which of the statements given above are correct?
A. Only I and II
B. Only I, II and III
C. Only II, III and IV
D. All of I, II, III and IV
E. Only I and IV
ANSWER: B
SOLUTION: <b>✅ Correct answer: B – Only I, II and III</b>

<b>🔎 Statement-wise analysis</b>
<table><tr><th>Statement</th><th>Verdict</th><th>Explanation</th></tr><tr><td><b>I</b></td><td>✅ True</td><td>RRBs were set up under the <b>Regional Rural Banks Act, 1976</b> (the first five began on <b>2 October 1975</b> through an Ordinance).</td></tr><tr><td><b>II</b></td><td>✅ True</td><td>Ownership is <b>Central Government 50% : State Government 15% : Sponsor Bank 35%</b>.</td></tr><tr><td><b>III</b></td><td>✅ True</td><td>The <b>Narasimham Working Group (1975)</b> recommended creating RRBs.</td></tr><tr><td><b>IV</b></td><td>❌ False</td><td>RRBs are <b>regulated by the RBI</b> and <b>supervised by NABARD</b>. SEBI regulates the securities market, not banks.</td></tr></table>

<b>📌 Key pointers</b>
<ul><li><b>Objective:</b> credit and banking services for <u>small and marginal farmers, agricultural labourers, artisans and small entrepreneurs</u> in rural areas.</li><li><b>Idea:</b> combine the <i>local feel</i> of cooperative banks with the <i>professionalism</i> of commercial banks.</li><li>The <b>first RRB</b> was <b>Prathama Bank</b> (Moradabad, Uttar Pradesh), sponsored by Syndicate Bank – <mark>2 October 1975</mark>.</li><li>RRBs have been merged (amalgamated) over the years – from about <b>196 in 2005</b> to far fewer today under the <i>"One State – One RRB"</i> approach. Check the latest count in current affairs.</li></ul>

<b>📊 Ownership of an RRB</b>
<table><tr><th>Owner</th><th>Share</th></tr><tr><td>Central Government</td><td><b>50%</b></td></tr><tr><td>State Government</td><td><b>15%</b></td></tr><tr><td>Sponsor Bank (a commercial bank)</td><td><b>35%</b></td></tr></table>

<b>⚠️ Don't get confused!</b> The <i>Narasimham Working Group (1975)</i> is about <b>RRBs</b>. The <i>Narasimham Committees (1991 and 1998)</i> are about <b>banking-sector reforms</b>. The chairman, <b>M. Narasimham</b>, was the same person, but the subjects are different.

<b>⚡ Memory trick:</b> <mark>50 – 15 – 35 = Centre – State – Sponsor bank.</mark> Regulator = <b>RBI</b>, Supervisor = <b>NABARD</b>.
